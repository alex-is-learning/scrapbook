# 2 - What's actually true
## 2a - Other people live in the same fundamental uncertainty that I live in
- Something here about how I don't fully get that other people have the same experience as me, with specific regard to how they *also* don't have access to like, The Truth, or some unassailable set of values that are watertight, or Absolute Certainty or whatever
- A few threads that come to mind here:
	1. One of David Chapman's main points in Meaningness is that meaning isn't permanent (eternal/eternalism) but is nebulous (and patterned).
	2. John Boyd and the epistemological shift of the ~1960s re: fundamental uncertainty, unknowability (e.g. Gödel's Incompleteness Theorem, Chaos Theory, Heisenberg's uncertainty principle)
	3. That philosopher who's like idk, "fundamental uncertainty" (ah that's it, John Dewey's "The Quest for Certainty")
	4. Chaos/complexity/that thing Jordan Peterson wrote
		- Peterson & Flanders 2002: [Complexity Management Theory: Motivation for Ideological Rigidity and Social Conflict](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/11235376_Complexity_Management_Theory_Motivation_for_Ideological_Rigidity_and_Social_Conflict)[^3]
- They have the same phenomenology as we, which includes a lack of access to any kind of Ground Truth. There's no Platonic realm of forms or perfect philosophical system that someone can install and become Perfect
## 2b - *Even the better educated people 
- Let's take the history professor
	- There are still many things they don't know
	- They could still be wildly wrong about many things
	- They could still be wildly myopic, have huge blindspots, huge shadows
- It's realising that it's moha
	- The three poisons (kleshas) in buddhism are craving, aversion, and ~delusion/confusion (moha)
	- I model people as if they *aren't* confused/deluded. But of course, they are! Unless they're someone like David Chapman, maybe. Or Dan Brown. Let's say there are n Buddhas. 1000 let's say. Then it's fair to say that 99.9999% of people are fundamentally confused
## 2c - *Even the better raised people*
- Say you have two awesome parents who you really like. Let's say you're Conan O'Brien's son. You have two great parents, a sister, you're wealthy. You have good role models, a good social scene etc
- You still have moha, confusion/delusion. You still were born without acces to Ground Truth, still live in Modernity with a thousand competing narratives competing for your attention, still exist in "The Meaning Crisis" (Vervaeke)
- I mean, I'm realising that the Buddha is a great analogue here. He was literally a Prince. Literally in a better situation even than Conan O'Brien's son if you can believe that. And he still was shocked by the suffering in the world[^4]
# 3 - Where it has come from / when it started showing up
## Modernity 
- Oh, it's worth remembering that, haha lmao again, it'd be easy for me to do a thing here of like "yes it's just *me* who is doing this and it's *my fault*". No one else does this, just me, I'm so dumb

- A friend introduced me to "Self / Other / World" as a framework, and I think that's very relevant here
## Where this comes from: Self
### Uncertainty is costly, draining
- Going too deep into uncertainty = you'd probably get depressed. Everything's too confusing etc, or pointless
- If you're certain that Effective Altruism is the best movement for doing good, then you'll feel much better than if you're not sure...
### Working class / broken home
- A sense of, other people had parents who were role models, and/or a healthy family environment to teach them things
- And as I didn't quite have this, I've had to pick stuff up myself, and it's been an arduous imperfect process, and I'm still missing lots of stuff, and maybe will never catch up with someone who just straight up has great parents/siblings, etc
## Where this comes from: Other
- Hey, you know how we all live in fundamental uncertainty? 
- Well, a really efficient way to accrue power/influence is to be a Certainty Peddler 
- Religions come to mind as an obvious example
- But I think it's much more subtle, I think individual people ofc also want to project their Certainty rather than humbly admitting to uncertainty and relative powerlessness
- Politicians are an example analogue here, where projecting certainty is very important
- But I still think it's more quotidian and insidious. It's more like, idk... the judgemental family member who is certain that x is bad, the podcaster who...
- I think it's like, it's costly to signal uncertainty. Better to be a Devout Effective Altruist than to be half-in, or very aware of the limitations
## Where this comes from: World
- "Other" already covers how e.g. Modernity includes subtle/invisible (doxa) pressures to appear Certain, Competent etc. The youtuber needs to have total conviction in their stance, the theory developer needs to be sure that they're onto something that will Change Everything. This is actually a useful, energy-giving posture a lot of the time, youthful naïveté or something
- We don't live in a culture of humility. We live in a culture of blustering, posturing, arrogance (see Trump)
