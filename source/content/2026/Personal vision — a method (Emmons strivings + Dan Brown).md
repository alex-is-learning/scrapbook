# What this is
- A method to do what Dan Brown recommends: develop a personal vision and operate from it
- Stage 1 comes from Robert Emmons, *The Psychology of Ultimate Concerns* (1999), Appendix A (pages 181 to 190) and Appendix B (page 191 onward)
- Stages 2 and 3 are not in that book. Dan gives them in one sentence each. The detail here is my reduced version, not a tested protocol
- Time: about 50 minutes for draft 1, then 5 minutes each week

# Stage 1 — collect the raw material (Emmons)
## Step 1. Write 15 strivings (20 minutes)
- Complete this sentence 15 times: "I typically try to ___"
- Emmons's rules
	- Write what you try to do, whether or not you succeed
	- Write no trait words (friendly, honest, intelligent)
	- Write no striving that you think you "ought" to have
	- Include things that you try to avoid or prevent
	- Do not compare yourself with other people
	- Look in four areas: work, home and family, relationships, leisure
- His examples: "trying to help others in need of help", "trying to seek new and exciting experiences", "trying to avoid feeling inferior to others"
- A striving can be broad ("make others happy") or specific ("make my partner happy")

## Step 2. Rate each striving (10 minutes)
- Importance, 0 to 5: how important is this striving in your life? (0 = not at all, 5 = extremely)
- Then four reasons. For each reason, write: not at all / a little / about half / mostly / completely
	- **Extrinsic** — someone else wants it, or you get a reward, praise or approval for it
	- **Introjected** — you feel ashamed, guilty or anxious if you do not do it
	- **Identified** — you believe that it is important and you agree with it freely
	- **Intrinsic** — the activity itself is interesting and enjoyable
- Emmons has 17 ratings. These 5 are the ones that help to write a vision. The other 12 measure progress, effort and difficulty

## Step 3. Mark the three themes (5 minutes)
- Emmons sorts strivings into 12 categories. Three of them consistently relate to well-being
	- **Intimacy** — closeness with particular people
	- **Generativity** — to give to the next generation, to leave something behind
	- **Spirituality** — oriented toward the sacred, toward something larger than the self
- Put I, G or S next to each striving that fits. Many strivings fit none

# Stage 2 — shape the vision (Dan)
## Step 4. Select the strivings that are yours
- Keep the strivings that score high on importance, identified and intrinsic
- Put a question mark on each striving that is mostly extrinsic or introjected. These are the "ought" items
- Look at which themes appear and which are absent

## Step 5. Write draft 1 (15 minutes)
- Dan's question: what is "the larger vision of your life", the deepest source of meaning and satisfaction?
- Write one paragraph that answers it. Use only the strivings that you kept
- Second question from Dan: what do you want to pass down to future generations?
- Draft 1 will be rough. Dan: "shape and reshape and shape that view many times until it's really clear to you"

## Step 6. Reshape each week (5 minutes)
- Read the paragraph once a week and change what is not true
- Stop when the paragraph does not change for three weeks
- Repeat steps 1 to 3 after a few months if the paragraph stops feeling correct

# Stage 3 — operate from it (Dan)
## Step 7. Keep it in the backdrop
- Dan: "every moment you're informed by that vision. It's always in the backdrop of your awareness"
- Read the paragraph each morning
- Before a decision, ask: does this serve the vision?
- Dan's test is day-to-day conduct with the people you meet: "by the fruits ye shall know them"

# What I left out
- Emmons's other 12 ratings, and his conflict grid (Part 3), which shows where one striving harms another. Use the grid if two kept strivings pull in opposite directions
- The full 12-category coding manual. It is for researchers who score other people's lists
